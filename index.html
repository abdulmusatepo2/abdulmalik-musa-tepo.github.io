🔬 Research Abstract

Title: MR2 Retention and Timeliness: A Retrospective Cohort Study at Marsabit County Referral Hospital, Kenya

Background: The Second Year of Life (2YL) is an important retention point in childhood immunisation. In Kenya, measles-rubella vaccine is given as MR1 at 9 months and MR2 at 18 months. Maintaining continuity between doses can be challenging in Arid and Semi-Arid Lands (ASAL), where geographic dispersion and population mobility complicate service access; facility-level evidence in northern Kenya remains limited.

Objective: To determine MR1-to-MR2 dropout rates and MR2 timeliness and describe temporal patterns in retention and delay across three study periods.

Methods: A retrospective cohort analysis of routine immunisation records was conducted at Marsabit County Referral Hospital's Maternal and Child Health clinic. Children aged >23 months at review and born up to July 2024 were eligible. Of 147 folders reviewed (50 in 2022, 48 in 2023 and 49 in 2024), 63 children with documented MR1 formed the analysis cohort; remaining records were classified by documented register disposition. Birth months clustered in December 2022, June-August 2023 and January-February 2024. Systematic sampling was impracticable in the busy clinical setting, so convenience sampling was used. Administration on or before the scheduled 18-month date was on-time; administration ≥1 day after was delayed. Descriptive statistics were primary, with exploratory Fisher's exact tests for residence and sex.

Results: Among 63 documented MR1 recipients, 36 had verifiable MR2 completion evidence (57.1%; 95% Wilson CI 44.9%-68.6%), while 27 (42.9%; 95% Wilson CI 31.4%-55.1%) had no verifiable MR2 completion in the reviewed facility record. Six records marked fully immunised lacked a specific verifiable MR2 entry and were not counted as confirmed completions. Cohort completion was 42.1% in 2022, 72.2% in 2023 and 57.7% in 2024. All 36 confirmed completers had usable dates: 15 (41.7%) were on-time and 21 (58.3%) delayed. Median delay was 4 days (mean 36.4; range 1-258); five exceeded 60 days. Exploratory comparisons detected no statistically significant association with residence (p=1.00) or sex (p=0.612).

Conclusion: Verifiable MR2 retention was incomplete and varied across the sampled cohorts, supporting strengthened MR1-to-MR2 tracking, timely follow-up, improved dose-specific documentation and context-responsive retention strategies.
